# Students as users of course software (US undergraduate and graduate)

As of: 15 November 2026

## Key takeaways

- **Students are deadline-driven and cross-course.** Their first question is "what is due, and where do I submit it?", not "what is in this course?". The best evidence of this is indirect: complaints about finding work, and a controlled study showing that targeted deadline alerts cut missed assignments.
- **Grades and comments compete, and grades win.** Where the grade is visible with the comments, many students attend to the grade first. The best experimental evidence (St Andrews, 2021) found that withholding the mark until after feedback raised later performance and feedback viewing. The sample was small and academically strong, so confidence is medium.
- **A grade preview in the Home feed is a risk, not a proven harm.** I found no study that tests grade previews in feeds. The nearest evidence points one way: the grade is the dominant signal and can crowd out the comments. Canvas itself makes scores in notifications opt-in. See "The Home grade-preview question" below.
- **Proof of submission is a recurring real need.** Canvas community threads show repeated requests for a submission receipt, and staff report students who believed they had submitted but had not. That is self-selected evidence, but it is consistent.
- **Students are working, not full-time learners.** About 70 percent of US college students work while enrolled (Georgetown CEW, 2015, older data). Design for short, interrupted sessions, often on a phone.

## Evidence quality: read this first

I could not retrieve the 2024 or 2025 EDUCAUSE Students and Technology reports. The EDUCAUSE library pages returned HTTP 403, and search snippets did not show the LMS or device tables. So I give **no** current national LMS-use or mobile-share figure. Where I cite devices, the data are from 2021 or older, or from one campus. Other desk-research sections should not cite a 2024/2025 mobile share from this file.

Other limits:

- Much of the feedback literature is UK or Australian. It is not US data. Marking cultures differ (marks versus letter grades).
- Reddit threads did not come up in my searches. The "real complaints" below come from Canvas Community forums, university help pages, student newspapers and app-review aggregators. All are self-selected. People who post complaints are not a sample of students.
- Several results are secondhand summaries. I flag each one.

## 1. How students actually use an LMS

### Devices and mobile share

- A November 2021 EDUCAUSE QuickPoll found laptops were the primary device for 81% of students, and smartphones the top secondary device at 56% ([EDUCAUSE Review, 2023](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2023/1/the-evolving-landscape-of-students-mobile-learning-practices-in-higher-education)). I saw this only through a search summary, because the page itself returned 403. It is also pandemic-era data.
- The same 2023 article, as summarised, reports about three-quarters of students said mobile technology made coursework easier to reach, and over 60% said it improved communication with peers and instructors. The article draws on a university survey, so it is not a national figure.
- The 2018 WCET device study found laptops preferred across all academic purposes, with lower preference for mobile devices ([WCET](https://wcet.wiche.edu/frontiers/2018/06/12/student-device-preferences-for-online-course-access-and-multimedia-learning/)). Older still.
- Reading: phones are the **secondary, quick-check device**. Laptops do the heavy work (writing, uploading, quizzes). [est] Students probably check deadlines and grades on the phone and submit on the laptop. This is an inference. It needs confirmation in testing.

### LMS use and satisfaction

- In the 2018 ECAR study, three-quarters of students were satisfied or very satisfied with their institution's LMS ([EDUCAUSE 2018](https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/ecar-study-of-undergraduate-students-and-information-technology/2018/introduction-and-key-findings), via search summary). Over three-quarters said the LMS was used in most or all courses.
- At the University of Maryland (2024, one campus, 259 undergraduate respondents, 3.2% response rate), 71% said it was easy or very easy to access and use course materials ([UMD](https://it.umd.edu/analytics/educause-2024-survey-results/introduction), search summary). The response rate is very low, so treat this as indicative only.
- A secondary blog summary of the 2025 EDUCAUSE report says help access, group-project logistics and assignment upload account for much of student frustration, and that nearly a third of students still face digital exclusion, especially low-income, rural and first-generation students ([Ctrl+Learn summary](https://ctrlpluslearn.substack.com/p/beyond-numbers-how-leaders-should)). The summary gave no percentages for the frustration items and I could not confirm it against EDUCAUSE. Medium-low confidence, but it matches the other evidence here. The report landing page is [here](https://library.educause.edu/resources/2025/4/2025-educause-students-and-technology-report).

High satisfaction scores and high complaint volume coexist. Satisfaction surveys ask about the system overall. Complaints are about specific moments (finding, submitting, checking). Whiteboard should design for the moments.

## 2. Finding work and deadlines across courses

- Students struggle to locate readings and assignments, because instructors' organisation can differ sharply from the courses they took before ([CU Boulder guide](https://oit.colorado.edu/services/teaching-learning-applications/canvas/help/instructor-support/designing-courses-for-students)).
- A University of Minnesota Duluth page states that one of the main complaints about Canvas is that assignments are hard to find ([UMD Duluth](https://www.d.umn.edu/cla/faculty/troufs/Canvas/Canvas_overview.html)). It is an instructor page, not a survey.
- A student newspaper at Indiana University describes assignments scattered across Canvas areas, with professors organising pages differently ([Indiana Daily Student, 2021](https://www.idsnews.com/article/2021/02/iu-professors-technology-online-classes)). A Plaid Press piece says work buried in modules or discussions may not appear on the dashboard ([Plaid Press, 2022](https://theplaidpress.com/2022/09/23/we-never-thought-we-would-miss-google-classroom)). Both are anecdotal.
- Structural causes appear in Canvas Community threads: the Assignments link can be hidden from the course navigation ([Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Question-Forum/Published-Assignments-not-visible-to-Students-in-a-Published/m-p/633553), [Michigan KB](https://teamdynamix.umich.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/6313/Canvas-Assignments-Link-Not-Visible-to-Students-in-Navigation)), and the mobile To-Do list can fail ([Canvas Mobile Forum](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Mobile-Forum/To-Do-List-for-Assignments-Not-Working-For-Students/td-p/544617)).
- Institutions respond with advice to instructors (week-by-week modules, landing pages) ([UChicago](https://academictech.uchicago.edu/2024/04/09/optimize-your-canvas-course-structure-for-seamless-navigation)). That is a workaround for a design problem. It relies on every instructor doing it.

Implication: the answer to "where is it?" should not depend on instructor course-building skill. Whiteboard already has a calendar and planned fixed structure. The evidence supports one cross-course "what's due" view as the primary surface, with the same layout in every course.

## 3. Notifications: overload versus missed information

- The only controlled evidence I found: an IEEE-reported study of a mobile app that sent push notifications when a student was **missing a submission for an imminent deadline**. Across two experimental pilots it significantly reduced missed assignments, compared with control notifications about instructor announcements ([IEEE abstract via search](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9373909)). I saw only the abstract, through a library proxy. The coordinator could not open the IEEE page on 15 Nov 2026, so treat this as unconfirmed. Sample sizes were not visible.
- Claims that notification overload causes missed deadlines are mostly anecdotal or vendor-sourced. A news piece says defaults can flood inboxes in peak seasons ([Academic Jobs](https://www.academicjobs.com/student-news/canvas-jumpscare-in-higher-education-student-notifications-and-anxiety-23724)). That article has no data. An SMS vendor blog argues for reminders ([TextBolt](https://textbolt.com/blog/reduce-missed-academic-deadlines/)). That is marketing.
- Canvas defaults are conservative. At Illinois State, Due Date is a weekly summary and Course Content notifications are off ([Illinois State](https://ithelp.illinoisstate.edu/display/Technology/Manage+Your+Canvas+Notifications)). Universities advise students to keep only Due Date alerts ([Oregon State](https://blogs.oregonstate.edu/workingwithcanvas/category/notification-info)) and warn that turning everything off risks missed deadlines ([Texas State](https://canvas.txstate.edu/courses/1288098/modules/items/48296205)).
- Canvas Community threads show users trying to switch off specific noisy alerts, such as assignment-created notifications and missing-assignment alerts ([thread](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Question-Forum/how-to-disable-assignment-created-notification/m-p/658182), [thread](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Archived-Questions/ARCHIVED-Shut-off-missing-assignment-alert/td-p/224391)). Self-selected.
- App-store and review-site comments (Capterra, JustUseApp) mention excessive, delayed or confusing notifications, and notification taps that land on a loading screen or the home page rather than the item ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/127214/CANVAS/reviews/), [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/480883488/canvas-student/reviews)). Note: the aggregator data are partly old, and the overall App Store rating is high (4.7, per [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/ios/canvas-student/480883488?country=us)). Do not read these as typical.

Reading: **fewer, more specific, deep-linked alerts** is the supported direction. Evidence for "less is better" in general is weak. Evidence for "specific and actionable beats broad" is the one controlled result.

## 4. Procrastination, deadlines, time zones

- Steel's meta-analysis (2007, 691 sources) found the strongest predictors of procrastination are task aversiveness, task delay, low self-efficacy and impulsiveness, plus low conscientiousness ([Steel 2007](https://stafforini.com/works/steel-2007-nature-procrastination-metaanalytic/)). Delay is partly a feeling problem (an aversive task), not just a planning problem.
- Ariely and Wertenbroch (2002) found self-imposed deadlines help but less than external ones ([abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/ariely-2002-procrastination-deadlines-performance/)). Later working papers did not replicate the effect fully ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19874.pdf)). Evidence is contested. I would not build a feature on it.
- Reasonable inference [est]: many students submit close to the deadline, so the last hour is the highest-stakes moment for slow uploads, session timeouts and unclear confirmation. I did not find a US study that gives the share of submissions in the final hour. Do not cite a number.
- **Time zones:** I searched for student-side evidence on "11:59 pm" and time-zone confusion and found none. This is a gap. Online and traveling students are a plausible risk [est]. It is cheap to design for (show the due time with its zone) and worth asking test participants.

## 5. Submission anxiety and receipts

- Canvas shows an on-screen "Turned in!" confirmation with a green check and the submission time, and students can revisit the page to see it ([Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Ideas/Assignments-Submission-confirmation-email-for-students/idi-p/654522), thread descriptions). Canvas does not appear to send a receipt email by default, based on the community threads. I could not confirm this in official docs.
- Idea threads ask for a receipt email. One request says support staff often hear from students who thought they had submitted but had sent work to the wrong assignment or course ([Canvas Ideas](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Ideas/Notifications-Assignment-and-quiz-submission-notification/idi-p/602551)). Another reply says an email "removes a lot of doubt" ([Canvas Ideas](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Ideas/Assignments-Submission-confirmation-email-for-students/idi-p/654522)).
- Counterpoint in the same threads: an email from the same system is no more reliable than the in-system record. So the value is in **independent, portable proof** (a copy the student holds), not just a second notice.
- A CSU Dominguez Hills student tip tells students to verify their submission, which shows the problem is common enough to need a help article ([CSUDH](https://at.csudh.edu/canvas/?p=11982)).
- "Canvas ate my submission" complaints are a known pattern ([Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Archived-Questions/ARCHIVED-Are-you-tired-of-investigating-quot-Canvas-ate-my/td-p/113466)). Self-selected.

Whiteboard's immutable receipts directly address a documented need. The evidence supports: confirmation on screen, a retrievable receipt, and clear "which assignment, which course" wording.

## 6. Grades, grade anxiety and checking behaviour

- I found **no rigorous study of university students' grade-checking anxiety**. The material is K-12 news and student opinion pieces. A KQED report quotes a counselor saying there are no scientific studies of mental-health impacts ([KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/11714761/mental-health-experts-worry-digital-open-grade-books-are-stressing-kids-out)). A sociologist estimated about 10% "hypercheckers", an interview estimate, not a measurement ([KJZZ](https://kjzz.org/content/1868452/some-students-and-parents-are-hyperchecking-grades-why-could-be-problem)). High school, not college.
- An Oklahoma State psychologist, quoted in the same coverage, says portals are not inherently harmful, and the concern starts when checking becomes compulsive. Professionals suggest weekly rather than constant checking (KQED). Expert opinion, not trial evidence.
- Do not cite an "81 percent find grades stressful" figure. It came from a student paper summarising a school survey. Not reliable.

So for grade anxiety, the honest summary is: plausible, widely reported, weakly measured at college level. Design for calm, but do not claim it is proven.

## 7. Feedback uptake: grades versus comments

- **Butler (1987, 1988):** students given task-involving comments showed higher interest, and comments alone beat grades or grades plus comments on interest. Performance effects were less clear-cut. The 1988 sample was 5th and 6th graders, so it is not college data ([abstract](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/task-involving-and-ego-involving-properties-of-evaluation-effects/), [summary](https://www.edweek.org/education/opinion-grades-versus-comments-what-does-the-research-really-tell-us/2019/06)). The popular "comments beat grades" claim is cleaner than the data.
- **Kluger and DeNisi (1996):** 607 effect sizes, 23,663 observations. Feedback helped on average (d = 0.41) but reduced performance in over a third of cases. Effectiveness drops when attention shifts toward the self ([Psychological Bulletin, via summary](https://swov.nl/nl/publicatie/effects-feedback-interventions-performance-historical-review-meta-analysis-and)). Mostly not student samples.
- **Winstone et al. (2017):** a systematic review of 195 outputs (1985 to early 2014) on what affects whether learners use feedback. It argues learners must actively engage ("proactive recipience") ([Aston](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/supporting-learners-agentic-engagement-with-feedback-a-systematic/)). I could not check the full text, so I do not repeat its taxonomy.
- **Pitt and Norton (2017):** emotions shape whether students act on feedback ("emotional backwash"); negatively phrased feedback leads some students to respond differently, and anxiety can deepen low self-efficacy ([Kent repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/54055)). UK, qualitative.
- **Kuepper-Tetzel and Gardner (2021, *Psychology Learning & Teaching*):** Experiment 1 randomly assigned 116 second-year psychology students to grade-before-feedback or feedback-before-grade; feedback-first improved on a similar later assessment. Experiment 2 compared a third-year cohort (N = 97) with a historical control (N = 90) and found more students viewed feedback when marks were withheld ([St Andrews](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-temporary-mark-withholding-on-academic-performance/), [Glasgow eprint](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/234256)). Caveats: one UK university, psychology students, older and academically successful sample, and written reflection was part of the interventions, so withholding alone is hard to isolate.
- Student-reported reasons for skipping comments: a bad mark is too depressing to read, a good mark means no need ([Glasgow slides](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_403130_smxx.pdf), slide deck, secondhand).
- Platform support: Canvas post policies let instructors release comments before scores ([Manchester guide](https://www.elearning.fse.manchester.ac.uk/getting-started-with-canvas/?p=2174), [Canvas post policies](https://community.canvaslms.com/docs/DOC-17328)). Under a manual policy, students get no notification or view of the grade or score change ([LJMU](https://cm-prod.ljmu.ac.uk/edtech/staff-guides/canvas-assignments/grade-post-policies)).

## The Home grade-preview question

Whiteboard Home currently shows "Quiz 2 · 8 of 10". What the evidence does and does not say:

- **No direct evidence.** I found no study comparing grade previews in a feed with no preview. Anyone claiming one is guessing.
- **Indirect evidence says the score is the dominant signal.** Where a grade is visible with feedback, students tend to prioritise the grade over the comments (Kuepper-Tetzel and Gardner, citing Jackson and Marks 2016; I could not verify the latter directly). Feedback-first improved later performance and viewing in their data.
- **A score on Home is the strongest form of exposure.** It appears before the student chooses to open the feedback, and it is read at a glance, possibly in public or between classes. [est] A bad score in a feed gives no chance to frame it. This is inference.
- **Canvas treats scores in notifications as opt-in.** The "Include scores when alerting about grade changes" setting is a checkbox students must tick ([FSU guide](https://support.canvas.fsu.edu/kb/article/923-canvas-notification-settings-guide/), [Indiana](https://www.kb.indiana.edu/d/bfdl)). That suggests Instructure considers score-in-alert a preference, not a default. I could not confirm whether scores reach push notifications.
- **The case for showing it:** students do check grades, and hiding grades entirely may push them to ask staff or refresh. The one counter-argument I found says portals help students manage workload. Weak, opinion-level.
- **Conflict with a Whiteboard rule:** the rule "Graded is not Released" is sound. But if a score shows on Home after release, it can still preempt the comments. Release is necessary but not sufficient to protect feedback uptake. Medium confidence.

Suggested stance: Home shows "Feedback ready" with the item name. The score appears one tap in, beside the comments. Test it (see open questions).

## 8. Trust: "did it submit?"

Trust in a submission is built from four things in the community evidence: a visible confirmation at the moment of submission, a record that can be looked up later, a time that matches the student's clock, and clarity on which assignment it went to. The Canvas threads above show each of these failing. Truthful pending states matter. A spinner that never resolves is worse than an error.

## 9. First-generation, working and commuter students

- About 70% of college students work while enrolled. Around 40% of undergraduates and 76% of graduate students work at least 30 hours a week ([Georgetown CEW press release](https://cew.georgetown.edu/workinglearnerspressrelease), [newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/seventy-percent-of-college-students-work-while-enrolled-new-georgetown)). This is 2015 data, and the follow-up links long work hours to lower grades and completion ([CEW 2018](https://cew.georgetown.edu/resource/balancing-work-and-learning-implications-for-low-income-students-3)). Use the 70% figure with its date.
- ECAR 2018 reported that device access differs across groups, including first-generation and independent students ([ECAR](https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/enhancing-student-academic-success-with-technology/introduction-and-key-findings), search summary). The 2017-based report cautions that assuming students already know the technology can disadvantage first-generation and nontraditional students ([ECAR](https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/enhancing-student-academic-success-with-technology/conclusion-recommendations-acknowledgments)).
- I found no study of commuters and the LMS specifically.
- Reading [est]: these students have fragmented time, may rely on a phone or shared device, and are less likely to know unwritten norms ("where do I ask for an extension?"). Plain labels, no insider vocabulary, and a clear path to a human matter more for them.

## 10. Discussion boards and group work

- In one undergraduate discussion-forum study, 90.6% said they posted because it was required, and lurking and intimidation were key themes. Some lurkers still did well ([AJET](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/view/452)).
- In a survey of 131 second-year psychology students, anonymous posting made students significantly more likely to post ([Springer, 2022](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10639-022-11517-3)). Single university. Social anxiety is described as a major impediment to participation ([PMC, 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9933033)).
- Dennen cautions that not posting does not equal low engagement ([ascilite](https://blog.ascilite.org/1187-2/)).
- Group work: research on social loafing in online groups is thin. Piezon and Ferree (2008) surveyed 227 students ([IRRODL](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/download/484/1034?inline=1)), and Jassawalla et al. surveyed 349 business undergraduates on perceived loafing ([St. John Fisher](https://fisherpub.sjf.edu/business_facpub/132)). I found no clean prevalence figure. Group logistics were named as a top frustration in the secondary 2025 summary above.

## 11. Joining a course and first login

I found no study on the join-course or first-login journey in an LMS. That is a real gap for the prototype. Adjacent evidence: students are told to confirm their email to receive notifications ([example](https://helpdesk.tcsedsystem.edu/hc/en-us/articles/42878468468503-How-do-I-confirm-my-email-address-so-that-I-can-receive-Canvas-notifications-and-other-messages)), and unconfirmed email silently blocks notifications. [est] A student who joins and then receives nothing will assume nothing is due. This is the most important thing to test in the setup and join-course prototype.

## Design implications for Whiteboard

1. **Make one cross-course "What's due" list the primary student surface**, sorted by due time, showing course, item, due time and status. Tag: Student. Evidence: [CU Boulder](https://oit.colorado.edu/services/teaching-learning-applications/canvas/help/instructor-support/designing-courses-for-students), [UMD Duluth](https://www.d.umn.edu/cla/faculty/troufs/Canvas/Canvas_overview.html). Confidence: Medium (consistent but anecdotal).
2. **Keep the same course layout for every course.** Do not let instructors hide the Assignments entry point. Tag: Student, Instructor. Evidence: [Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Question-Forum/Published-Assignments-not-visible-to-Students-in-a-Published/m-p/633553). Confidence: Medium.
3. **Show the due time with its time zone and the date, in the student's own zone, with the course zone as a secondary label.** Tag: Student. Evidence: no student-side study found; this is cheap and low risk. Confidence: Low (no direct evidence). Test it.
4. **After submit, show a receipt page: item name, course, submission time with seconds and zone, a receipt ID, and what was received (file names, sizes).** Allow a copy or download. Tag: Student. Evidence: [Canvas Ideas](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Ideas/Notifications-Assignment-and-quiz-submission-notification/idi-p/602551). Confidence: High for the need, Medium for the exact fields.
5. **Make the receipt retrievable later from the item and from a "Submitted" list.** Tag: Student, TA. Evidence: [Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Canvas-Ideas/Assignments-Submission-confirmation-email-for-students/idi-p/654522). Confidence: Medium.
6. **Never show an indefinite spinner on submit.** Show truthful states: uploading, received, failed with a retry that does not lose files. Tag: Student. Evidence: [Canvas Community](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/Archived-Questions/ARCHIVED-Are-you-tired-of-investigating-quot-Canvas-ate-my/td-p/113466). Confidence: Medium.
7. **Default notifications to a small set of actionable alerts (due soon, not yet submitted, feedback ready), each deep-linked to the item.** Keep announcement and discussion alerts opt-in or digested. Tag: Student. Evidence: [IEEE abstract](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9373909), [Oregon State](https://blogs.oregonstate.edu/workingwithcanvas/category/notification-info). Confidence: Medium (one controlled study, abstract only).
8. **Do not show scores on Home or in alerts by default.** Show "Feedback ready" and open to the comments first, with the score beside them. Allow the student to opt in to scores in alerts. Tag: Student. Evidence: [Kuepper-Tetzel and Gardner 2021](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-temporary-mark-withholding-on-academic-performance/), [Canvas score opt-in](https://support.canvas.fsu.edu/kb/article/923-canvas-notification-settings-guide/). Confidence: Medium (small, high-achieving UK sample; inference for feeds).
9. **Support "feedback before score" as an instructor option at release,** including a prompt for the student to read comments first. Tag: Instructor, Student. Evidence: [Kuepper-Tetzel and Gardner](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/234256), [Manchester guide](https://www.elearning.fse.manchester.ac.uk/getting-started-with-canvas/?p=2174). Confidence: Medium.
10. **Word feedback surfaces neutrally. Do not use red, failure language or rank framing on a low score.** Tag: Student. Evidence: [Pitt and Norton 2017](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/54055), [Kluger and DeNisi 1996](https://swov.nl/nl/publicatie/effects-feedback-interventions-performance-historical-review-meta-analysis-and). Confidence: Medium.
11. **Make a "Graded, not yet released" state visible only as a neutral status ("Being reviewed"), with no score and no hint of one.** Tag: Student, TA. Evidence: Canvas manual policy hides scores and change alerts ([LJMU](https://cm-prod.ljmu.ac.uk/edtech/staff-guides/canvas-assignments/grade-post-policies)); grade-change worry ([Canvas Gradebook practice notes](https://cm-prod.ljmu.ac.uk/edtech/staff-guides/canvas-assignments/grade-post-policies)). Confidence: Medium.
12. **Design the quiz start and timer for a student who is anxious: show the window, the time limit and the late-start effect before they press Start.** Tag: Student. Evidence: none direct; inferred from submission-anxiety threads. Confidence: Low. Test it.
13. **Keep the phone view for checking and light actions (what's due, receipts, feedback reading). Do not depend on it for long uploads.** Tag: Student. Evidence: [EDUCAUSE Review 2023](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2023/1/the-evolving-landscape-of-students-mobile-learning-practices-in-higher-education), [WCET 2018](https://wcet.wiche.edu/frontiers/2018/06/12/student-device-preferences-for-online-course-access-and-multimedia-learning/). Confidence: Medium-Low (data are 2018 to 2021).
14. **Use plain labels and give a visible way to ask a person for help.** Tag: Student (first-generation, working). Evidence: [ECAR](https://www.educause.edu/ecar/research-publications/enhancing-student-academic-success-with-technology/conclusion-recommendations-acknowledgments). Confidence: Medium-Low.
15. **At join-course, confirm the notification channel and show what the student will be told, so they do not assume silence means nothing is due.** Tag: Student. Evidence: [example](https://helpdesk.tcsedsystem.edu/hc/en-us/articles/42878468468503-How-do-I-confirm-my-email-address-so-that-I-can-receive-Canvas-notifications-and-other-messages). Confidence: Low (inference).
16. **Discussions: offer a lower-pressure way to take part (reading marked as participation, optional anonymous questions where the instructor allows).** Tag: Student, Instructor. Evidence: [Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10639-022-11517-3), [AJET](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/view/452). Confidence: Low-Medium.
17. **Groups: show who submitted what and when, so free-riding disputes can be settled from a record.** Tag: Student, Instructor. Evidence: [IRRODL](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/download/484/1034?inline=1) (thin). Confidence: Low.

## Open questions for user testing

1. When a student sees "Feedback ready" with no score, do they open it? Or do they feel something is being hidden and get anxious? Compare with "Quiz 2 · 8 of 10" on Home.
2. What do students look at first on opening the app, on a phone and on a laptop? Is it deadlines, grades, or announcements?
3. After submitting, what makes a student believe it worked? Is a receipt page enough, or do they want an email or a screenshot?
4. Do students read the due time as their local time or the course's time? Have any been caught out by a time zone?
5. At join-course, what do students expect to happen next? What do they think "no notifications" means?
6. Which alerts would they keep on after a week, and which would they turn off?
